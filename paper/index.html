ABSTRACT

Turning historical search signals into editorial priorities.

This study investigates whether historical search and content-performance signals can be used to identify pages that should be prioritized for content review.

The analysis frames content decline as a binary classification problem. A page is labelled as declining when impressions in the final 30-day period fall below 80% of impressions in the preceding 30-day period.

A Random Forest model is trained using predictors calculated only from the preceding 30-day period. The final system produces a ranked content-opportunity score intended to help editors focus limited review capacity on higher-risk pages.

LIMITATIONS

What this analysis cannot claim.

01

The model identifies statistical associations and does not establish causal relationships.

02

A decline in impressions does not necessarily mean that a page requires a content refresh.

03

The 20% decline threshold affects the definition of the outcome.

04

Performance may differ on future data or clients with different characteristics.

05

Recommendations are decision-support outputs and should not automatically trigger content changes.

SOCIAL-POST CUT

Built a leakage-aware search-intelligence model that ranks content pages by estimated risk of future impression decline. Using historical performance signals and client-held-out validation, the Random Forest achieved a ROC AUC of 0.624 and 90% Precision@50. The output is designed as editorial decision support, not automatic content action.
